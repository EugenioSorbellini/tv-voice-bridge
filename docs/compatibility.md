# Compatibility contract

The wire adapter is currently tested against androidtvremote2 **0.3.2** and Home Assistant **2026.10.2**. Home Assistant owns that dependency; the integration does not introduce a competing package requirement. The minimum HA version is declared in hacs.json. CI uses the exact minimum HA and library versions so a current development environment cannot accidentally hide a compatibility break.

The installed Google Android TV Remote Service **7.00.956317615** on the tested TCL consumes protobuf field 31 as audio bytes; its PCM decoder passes those bytes through unchanged. Upstream 0.3.2 nests a session/samples message there, introducing metadata into the audio. In measured sessions with 8192-byte chunks that wrapper was five bytes, shifting PCM16 alignment; wrapper size is not universally fixed. `protocol.py` adapts voice traffic only on the bridge's dedicated connection. Generated `voice.proto` schemas explicitly separate client audio bytes from the server's field-31 session-close message. It preserves pairing and the core integration connection. It declares 16 kHz, Android mono channel mask 16, PCM16 format 2.

Live tests on 2026-10-22 confirmed recognition with raw PCM at both 8 and 16 kHz. The receiver defaults to 8 kHz/mono/PCM16, and raw 8 kHz works without explicitly declaring those audio fields. The bridge's 16 kHz choice is therefore not a demonstrated TCL requirement. Receiver inspection supports a Google Remote Service implementation explanation rather than a discovered TCL-specific parsing branch; other vendors and service generations remain unverified. One nested 16 kHz trial also recognized the phrase, demonstrating that recognition can tolerate malformed input, not that the nested schema matches this receiver.

This adapter accesses private protocol internals and is deliberately isolated and version-gated. If Home Assistant upgrades androidtvremote2, the integration reports `unsupported_version` rather than silently applying the patch to an untested implementation. Updating support requires reviewing upstream framing and callbacks, running the real VoiceStream framing and shutdown tests, and testing recognition on supported hardware. The remaining private hooks are the dedicated remote protocol reference and its send/receive methods; shutdown also captures its transport and `on_con_lost` future before upstream clears them. Prefer removing this adapter after an upstream fix is available; do not extend its version allowlist without evidence.

The one-second pre-search delay remains a policy constant, not evidence that every Android TV needs the same timing. On the tested TV, immediate voice requests intermittently get no voice-begin reply, and 0.25–0.5 s delays can lose the start of speech without any error. Polling `is_voice_enabled` does not help because it is already true at connection, and waiting for the initial app/IME callback also failed once in three. The bridge waits for the TV's voice-begin reply before audio.

The v1.1.0 lifecycle implementation removes the fixed 15-second processing grace. Submission sends the final audio and client voice-end, immediately calls graceful transport close, and awaits `on_con_lost`. A five-second **failure deadline**, rather than a minimum wait, aborts a stalled close and reports delivery failure. The stop reply waits for shutdown, and the TV stays reserved while its connection is draining. Cancellation and unload also own this bounded cleanup, including connections that finish startup after cancellation. Successful shutdown is not recognition confirmation.

A matching server field-31 close stops further audio, discards any unsent partial chunk and releases the upload handler. Stale, missing-session and duplicate closes do not end a different session. The bridge does not echo client voice-end after the TV has already closed voice. The owning client receives `session_ended` on its next progress/stop request; this is not an assistant-completion event. Already accepted transport bytes cannot be recalled. The card streams its recording to Home Assistant while capturing, but the bridge holds that audio and forwards it to the TV only on submission, as the burst path validated in trials 35–36. A TV close therefore surfaces to the card through its next progress acknowledgement rather than a pushed microphone-stop notification.

Other device reports should include model and Remote Service version without speech content. Hardware recognition compatibility is verified only on the tested TCL installation.
